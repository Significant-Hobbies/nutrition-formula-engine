## 2. Final result

### 2.1 What is known and what is assumed

- Before completing the two missing profiles, **99.429%** of explicitly
  quantified non-water input mass had usable nutrient profiles and **0.571%**
  did not.
- The remaining 0.571% has been completed using two visible assumptions:
  `NEPAZENE` is sodium methylparaben (1 kg; 0.190%) and orange flavour is a
  non-nutritive flavour mixture at 1.0 g/mL (2 kg equivalent; 0.381%).
- The calculation therefore has **100% profile coverage under the stated
  assumptions**. This means every formula input has a calculation profile; it
  does not mean 100% laboratory certainty.
- Iron, zinc, vitamin potency, actual final yield, and process retention remain
  sources of product uncertainty even though the table is numerically complete.

### 2.4 Accuracy statement

- Five marketed-product salt-to-active comparisons produced **0.244% mean
  absolute percentage error** and **0.604% maximum observed error**. This
  validates arithmetic and molecular conversion, not supplier identity or lot
  potency.
- Product-specific quantified uncertainty is currently available for iron and
  zinc. Vitamin potency, actual yield, process loss, stability, sampling, and
  laboratory-method uncertainty are not quantified.
- The result is numerically complete because explicit assumptions fill the
  missing profiles. It becomes evidence-complete only after those assumptions
  are confirmed by supplier records and finished-product testing.

## 3. Assumptions and confirmation requirements

### Assumptions used to complete the calculation

1. Finished volume is exactly 1,000 L at the relevant measurement temperature.
2. `NEPAZENE` is **sodium methylparaben**, the first-ranked candidate, with
   molecular formula `C8H7NaO3`; its sodium contribution is calculated from
   PubChem CID 23663626 molecular mass data.
3. Orange means a flavour mixture rather than juice or concentrate, has density
   1.0 g/mL, and contributes zero reportable standard macros/micros.
4. `NEPASOL` is neutral propylparaben/Nipasol rather than sodium propylparaben.
5. Ferric ammonium citrate is the brown grade with a nominal 19.5% iron assay.
6. Zinc sulphate is the monohydrate for the nominal value.
7. Cyanocobalamin, folic acid, and pyridoxine HCl are pure raw materials rather
   than diluted or potency-standardized premixes.
8. Sugar is sucrose; xanthan gum is fully dietary fibre.
9. No process degradation, retention loss, overage, evaporation, or sampling
   variation has been applied.
10. Per-100-g values are unavailable until finished-product density is supplied.

### Effect if the leading assumptions are wrong

- If `NEPAZENE` is neutral methylparaben rather than sodium methylparaben,
  sodium falls from approximately **61.065 mg to 47.862 mg per 100 mL**.
- If `NEPAZENE` is a paraben blend, its individual parabens cannot be reported
  until the blend specification is supplied.
- If the orange flavour carrier contains carbohydrate, alcohol, fat, vitamins,
  or minerals, the corresponding results must be updated. Its assumed mass is
  only 0.2 g per 100 mL, which bounds its total mass contribution but does not
  prove micronutrient absence.

### Evidence needed to replace assumptions

- Supplier label/specification and lot CoA for `NEPAZENE` and `NEPASOL`.
- Orange flavour carrier composition and density.
- Ferric ammonium citrate grade and measured iron assay.
- Zinc sulphate hydrate, purity, and assay.
- Vitamin potency/premix specifications.
- Actual final batch volume, temperature, density, yield, and weighing
  tolerances.
- Finished-product laboratory results with method uncertainty.

This report should be shared as a complete stated-assumption theoretical
composition, not as a certificate of analysis, final food label, or regulatory
declaration.
